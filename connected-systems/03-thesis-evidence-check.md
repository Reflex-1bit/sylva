# 3. The thesis, checked claim by claim

The consciousness thesis is the reason the three products exist. If it is wrong, the products are still useful but the story around them is not. So this chapter does the uncomfortable work: it puts each claim next to what the evidence says. The brief asked for truth over helpfulness, and that is what this chapter tries to deliver.

Method. Five research agents searched for primary sources. The network proxy in this session blocked the publisher, encyclopedia and government pages, so the agents verified claims through several independent search results that quoted the source text, and could not read any primary source end to end. That is why nothing in this chapter is tagged [Certain]. [Likely] means several independent results agreed on the citation and figures. [Guessing] means it could not be checked this session. Full citations are in `SOURCES.md`; keys like [Rathje 2021] point there. Sources older than two years carry their year.

## 3.1 Scorecard

| Verdict | Claims |
|---|---|
| **Holds** | Engagement ranking selects for anger and out-group content. Re-ranking changes polarisation. Alcohol is far more harmful than psychedelics on the best harm ranking. Cannabis potency has risen sharply. Situations and hierarchies shape atrocity. Bread and circuses, Bengal, apartheid are real examples. Media capture is a well-known thesis. Consciousness is deeply unsolved. The transparency demand is already partly law. |
| **Needs rewording** | "Empathy is dying" (it fell to 2009 and has risen since). "Deliberate" short-circuiting of empathy (selection by metric, not shown intent). "Learned helplessness" (metaphor; evidence is correlational). "Frequency and energy are the same" (proportional, for photons). "Shamans revered, now pathologised" (culture shapes voice-hearing; schizophrenia is real everywhere). "Love cannot be replicated" (AI language reads as empathic; whether that is empathy is philosophy). "Mind and body are separate; the soul connects them" (metaphysics, minority view). "AI can perfectly stimulate each person" (strong persuasion, contested personalisation gains). "Divide and rule" (widely interpreted as such). |
| **Wrong as stated** | The quantum framing (inverted, and "observer" is not consciousness). "Tripping proves the energy layer of perception." "Socialist countries preserve nature better." |
| **Untestable as written** | Music translates the energy of a place. We are the universe. We inspire ourselves, we are the gods. The utopian counterfactual. These are the thesis's poetry, and poetry is not the problem. Mixing them with the empirical claims is. |

## 3.2 Part 1: conditioning and the illusion of choice

**Algorithms select for fear, anger and tribalism.** Mostly holds. Each moral-emotional word in a tweet raised its spread by about 20 percent across 563,312 tweets [Brady 2017]. Each word about the political out-group raised shares by about 67 percent across 2.7 million posts, the strongest predictor of engagement found [Rathje 2021]. A direct audit of X's engagement ranking against a chronological feed found it amplified angry and out-group-hostile political content, and users did not prefer it [Milli 2025]. Anger and tribalism are documented. Fear is not separately measured in this literature; outrage and threat overlap but are not the same [Guessing].

**"Deliberate, not accidental."** Overstated. The studies show selection by a metric. They do not show a plan to destroy empathy. The outcome is the same, and the European Commission's 2026 preliminary findings against TikTok and Meta do treat "addictive design" as a design choice the companies are responsible for [EC 2026]. Say "chosen metric, known effect" rather than "deliberate".

**Learned helplessness from the feed.** A metaphor, not a finding. Doomscrolling is a measurable construct [Sharma 2022] and daily media consumption tracks same-day depression and trauma symptoms in diary data [Price 2022], but both are self-report and correlational. The closest population figure: 40 percent of people across 48 markets sometimes or often avoid the news, and one in five of those cite feeling powerless to act [Reuters 2025]. That is survey evidence of the feeling, not of a conditioned helplessness.

**Postman and WALL-E forecast it.** Standard references; not verified this session [Guessing]. Amusing Ourselves to Death (1985) is a 41-year-old book and reads as a thesis about television, not algorithms.

**AI will train each person's perspective and perfectly stimulate them toward consumption.** Direction right, "perfectly" wrong. GPT-4 given basic demographic data had about 80 percent higher odds of shifting agreement in live debates than human debaters did [Salvi 2025]. But microtargeted language-model messages were not significantly more persuasive than generic ones in a study of about 8,500 people [Hackenburg 2024], and model scale gives diminishing returns for single messages [Hackenburg 2025]. Meta has said it aims to let brands create and target ads fully with AI by the end of 2026 [Likely, via press]. So: strong and improving persuasion, with the gain from personalisation real in conversation and small or absent in one-shot messaging.

**Cognitive offloading: human thought dumbed down to mimic AI.** Suggestive, not conclusive. A survey of 666 UK participants found AI use correlated with lower self-reported critical thinking [Gerlich 2025], but it is cross-sectional and a correction was later published. The widely shared "Your Brain on ChatGPT" is a preprint with 54 participants, 18 in the key session, and has drawn methodological criticism [Kosmyna 2025]. The feedback loop the thesis describes is plausible and unproven.

## 3.3 Part 2: economics and behavioural capture

**"Economics is probably the strongest behavioural science, stronger than psychology."** Needs rewording. Behavioural economics is applied cognitive psychology: prospect theory came from two psychologists [Kahneman and Tversky 1979] and Nudge from an economist and a lawyer building on it [Thaler and Sunstein 2008]. Psychology's replication crisis is real: of 100 studies from three journals, about 36 percent replicated with significant results [Open Science Collaboration 2015]. But the applied economics literature has the same problem. A 2022 meta-analysis of 447 nudge effects found a moderate average effect [Mertens 2022]; a re-analysis of the same data found severe publication bias and no evidence of an average effect after correcting for it [Maier 2022]. The thesis's real point survives: economics formalised the loop between stimulus and purchase at a scale psychology never did. "Strongest" does not.

**"We are consuming ourselves to death": abundance versus engineered scarcity.** The abundance half holds, and the thesis's number is right for one country. The world wasted 1.05 billion tonnes of food at retail, food-service and household level in 2022, 19 percent of food available to consumers, on top of about 13 percent lost in the supply chain [UNEP 2024]. The United States alone had 73.9 million tons of unsold or uneaten food in 2023 and 70 million tons in 2024, which is 148 and 140 billion pounds respectively [ReFED 2024; ReFED 2026]. So "about 150 billion pounds" is the US figure, not the world's, and should be cited as such. Clothing: garment production passed 100 billion a year in 2014 [McKinsey 2016]; the equivalent of a truckload of textiles is landfilled or burned every second [Ellen MacArthur Foundation 2017]; one fast-fashion retailer was adding 2,000 to 10,000 new items a day in late 2021 [Rest of World 2021]. The European Commission estimates 250,000 to 600,000 tonnes of unsold clothing are destroyed in Europe each year, and from 19 July 2026 large companies are banned from doing so [ESPR 2024]. The "engineered" half, that scarcity is manufactured to justify unequal comfort, is an argument, and a reasonable one, not a measured finding.

**Consumer money funds the harms we condemn.** Holds for the examples given. The Democratic Republic of Congo produced about 76 percent of the world's mined cobalt in 2024 and about 73 percent in 2025 [USGS 2025; USGS 2026]. Amnesty International documented children as young as seven mining cobalt by hand and traced the supply chain to major electronics brands [Amnesty 2016]; Cobalt Red is the recent book-length account [Kara 2023]. The thesis's claim that "we give more to the machine than its open supporters do" is arithmetic that no study was found to have done [Guessing]; it is plausible for anyone who owns a phone.

**Tourism as neo-colonisation with softer packaging.** The feeling is documented and the mechanism is measured; the label is rhetoric. Spain's anti-tourism protests began in the Canary Islands in April 2024, reached Barcelona in July 2024, and were coordinated across southern Europe in June 2025; Barcelona received 15.5 million tourists in 2024 against 1.7 million residents [Likely, via press]. In Barcelona's most Airbnb-intensive neighbourhoods, rents rose 7 percent and sale prices 17 percent between 2012 and 2016 because of short-term rentals [García-López 2020]; a US-wide study found a 1 percent rise in listings raised rents by 0.018 percent and prices by 0.026 percent at the median [Barron 2021]. Bali announced a moratorium on converting farmland to hotels and villas in September 2025 after deadly floods, and Thailand closed Maya Bay from 2018 to 2022 to let coral recover [Likely]. "Tourism measurably raises housing costs for residents" holds. "Neo-colonisation" is the thesis's judgment.

**"Comfort is not betterment; capitalism claims experience requires consumption."** Partly testable, and the evidence leans the thesis's way with a caveat. People report more happiness from experiential than material purchases of similar cost [Van Boven and Gilovich 2003]. Spending on others raised happiness more than spending on oneself in a 2008 study whose field experiment had 46 participants [Dunn 2008]; a registered replication with 712 participants found the effect, smaller [Aknin 2020]. The caveat: these studies compare kinds of spending. They do not show that experience is possible without spending, which is the thesis's stronger claim and remains an argument.

**The degrowth frame.** The thesis's "re-route consumption" is a degrowth idea, and it should know its own literature and critics. Less is More argues rich economies should plan a reduction in material throughput while expanding public services [Hickel 2020]; Doughnut Economics argues for meeting everyone's needs within ecological limits while being agnostic about growth, and Amsterdam adopted the model in April 2020 [Raworth 2017]; Prosperity Without Growth made the decoupling argument in 2009 [Jackson 2009]. The mainstream critique is that keeping emissions down without growth would require pushing everyone above the world mean income down to it, which is politically impossible [Milanović 2021]. The thesis does not have to resolve this. It should cite it.

**"Money is the single most important organising force"; "capitalism becomes the substitute religion".** Opinions, well put. No check applies.

## 3.4 Part 3: perception, the quantum analogy and energy

**"A particle when not observed, a light wave when observed."** Wrong as stated, and backwards. In the double-slit experiment the wave-like interference pattern appears when no which-path information is recorded, and the particle-like pattern appears when it is [Strassler 2025; arXiv 2016]. "Measurement" in physics means any interaction that records information, including a stray photon or ordinary decoherence; it does not require a conscious observer. The idea that consciousness causes collapse is a minority interpretation from the 1930s and 1960s [SEP, Likely]. Qubits do not show that observer and observed are one; they show superposition and decoherence, which is why quantum computers are shielded from all interaction, not from human attention. The thesis says this needs fact-checking. It does, and it fails. The sentence can be cut without losing anything the rest of the thesis depends on.

**"Frequency and energy are the same."** Needs rewording. A photon's energy is proportional to its frequency (E equals h times f). They are proportional, not the same, and for sound or water waves energy depends mainly on amplitude [IOP, Likely]. It is true that the senses transduce physical energy: light, pressure waves, chemicals, heat. "Absorbing the frequencies of a room" is a metaphor, and a good one, but it is not physics.

**"Tripping proves the energy layer of perception."** Wrong as stated. No study measures an energy layer. What the research does show is more interesting for this project: in a prospective survey of 654 people, nature relatedness rose two weeks, four weeks and two years after a psychedelic experience, mediated by the quality of the experience and its setting [Kettner 2019]. A tiny study (seven patients, seven controls) found the same plus decreased authoritarian views, with the authors warning against causal claims [Lyons 2018]. Clinically, psilocybin for depression is promising and mixed: no significant difference from escitalopram on the primary outcome at six weeks [Carhart-Harris 2021], large effects in a 24-person trial [Davis 2021], a 37 percent response at three weeks falling to 20 percent sustained at twelve weeks in a 233-person trial [Goodwin 2022]. The FDA rejected MDMA-assisted therapy in August 2024; a second Phase 3 psilocybin trial reported a smaller effect than its Phase 2 in February 2026 [Likely]. Rewrite as: psychedelics reliably alter perception and self-boundaries, increase felt connection to nature in surveys, and prove nothing about physics.

**"Consciousness remains the biggest unsolved question in science."** Holds in spirit. The hard problem was named in 1995 [Chalmers 1995]. In 2025 an adversarial collaboration tested the two leading theories against each other in about 256 participants and found no winner, challenging predictions of both [Cogitate 2025]. In 2023, 124 researchers called one of those theories pseudoscience in an open letter, which drew sharp rebuttals [Likely]. "Biggest" is rhetoric; "one of the deepest" is defensible.

**"Mind and body are two separate things; the soul is the connective thread."** This is Descartes' position from 1641. It is metaphysics, not a scientific claim, and it is a minority view: a majority of professional philosophers accept or lean toward physicalism about the mind in the 2020 PhilPapers survey, with the exact percentage (52 or 57 percent depending on cohort) unverified here [Bourget and Chalmers 2023]. The thesis is entitled to hold it. It should not present it as a finding.

**"What was once revered as spiritual perception is now pathologised."** Needs rewording. The evidence the thesis is reaching for is real: in matched groups of twenty patients each in the US, India and Ghana, Americans more often experienced voices as violent commands, while Indian and Ghanaian participants more often described relationships with their voices [Luhrmann 2015]. Culture shapes the experience. But all sixty were ill and seeking care, schizophrenia occurs in every culture, and the old finding of better outcomes in developing countries has been seriously questioned [Cohen 2008; Jablensky 2008]. "Revered then, pathologised now" is a romantic binary. "Culture shapes how voices are experienced and treated" is what the data supports.

**"Alcohol is normalised and legal despite its harms, while psychedelics are restricted."** Holds, with a caveat. On a 16-criterion multicriteria analysis alcohol scored 72, the highest overall harm of 20 drugs; LSD scored 7 and psilocybin mushrooms 6, the lowest [Nutt 2010]. The World Health Organization attributes 2.6 million deaths a year to alcohol against 0.6 million to other psychoactive drugs (2019 data, 2024 report) [WHO 2024]. The caveat: the overall harm score weights harm to others, which scales with how many people use a drug; it is not a per-user toxicity ranking, and the method has been criticised [Caulkins 2011].

**"Modern high-potency cannabis no longer serves the consciousness-expanding role."** Half holds. Mean THC in US seized samples rose from about 4 percent in 1995 to about 12 percent in 2014 and roughly 14 percent by 2019, with CBD falling [ElSohly 2016; ElSohly 2021]. Daily use of high-potency cannabis was associated with 4.8 times the odds of a psychotic disorder in a European case-control study [Di Forti 2019], a design that cannot establish causation. The "consciousness-expanding role" half is a value judgment and should be framed as one.

**"Socialist countries may preserve nature better."** Not supported as stated. Cuba's post-1991 forced shift to low-input farming is a real case of nature benefiting from de-growth by necessity, with persistent problems [Fernandez 2018]. Against it stand the Aral Sea, which lost about 90 percent of its surface area to Soviet cotton irrigation, Chernobyl, and Eastern Bloc industrial pollution [Likely]. No comparative study showing socialist systems outperform was found. The thesis's own repair is better than the claim: it is a consciousness issue, not an incentive issue, and that is true under any system.

## 3.5 Part 4: rhythm, algo-rhythms and the loss of empathy

**"The single thing that created civilisations, empathy, has been destroyed."** Not what the data says. The usual evidence is a 2011 meta-analysis of 13,737 American college students: empathic concern down 48 percent and perspective taking down 34 percent between 1979 and 2009 [Konrath 2011]. The same lead author's 2025 update, with 38,693 students and two nationally representative surveys, finds both measures rising since 2009 [Konrath 2025]. So empathy, as psychologists measure it, fell during the television and early internet decades and has been recovering during the algorithmic one. That does not prove the feed is harmless. It does mean "empathy is dying" should be retired as a claim.

**"Every algo-rhythm is different, so no shared reality, so hopelessness."** A hypothesis, and a good one. The mechanism (personalised ranking removes shared reference points) is plausible and the lever is real: re-ranking feeds by partisan animosity moved how warmly 1,256 X users felt toward the other party in a preregistered ten-day experiment [Piccardi 2025], and re-ranking the real feeds of about 9,400 adults reduced affective polarisation in two arms of the Prosocial Ranking Challenge, a March 2026 preprint [PRC 2026]. What the ranking does, a different ranking can undo. Chapter 4 builds on exactly this.

**Bread and circuses, divide and rule in India, apartheid.** Hold. Juvenal's Satire X (around 100 AD) is the source of "panem et circenses". The 1905 Partition of Bengal and the 1909 separate electorates are the standard examples of colonial divide and rule, widely interpreted that way though Curzon stressed administration; apartheid ran from 1948 to 1994 [Likely]. Soften "was" to "is widely read as".

**"Music is an art-simplification of our environment; it translates the energy of a place."** Untestable as written, and it does not need to be tested. The nearest evidence is that emotion and beauty are two of the five pathways to feeling connected to nature [Lumber 2017], which is a reason to build music and art into any reconnection product. Keep the claim as what it is: the author's inspiration.

## 3.6 Part 5: complicity and hypocrisy

**"Soldiers who commit atrocities are shaped by environment, training and chain of command."** Holds in substance, but the usual evidence is weak. Milgram's 65 percent obedience figure comes from one condition; archival work found that in over half of his 24 variations most participants disobeyed [Perry 2012]. The Stanford Prison Experiment is now regarded as unreliable: guards were coached [Le Texier 2019]. The better evidence is historical: most men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 were unexceptional and killed under group pressure and deference, while a minority refused without penalty [Browning 1992]. Arendt's "banality of evil" has been heavily revised; Eichmann was a committed ideologue who performed blandness at his trial [Stangneth 2014]. Current consensus: situations shape behaviour powerfully, and ideology and choice remain part of the picture. The thesis's "no moral authority" stance survives this; "conditioning, not individual evil" should become "conditioning and choice".

**Consumer money funds the harms we condemn (cobalt, fast fashion, oil, food systems).** See 3.3 for the verified figures on cobalt, clothing and food waste.

**Tourism as neo-colonisation with softer packaging.** See 3.3 for what the housing-price research shows.

**"An urban consumer with correct values harms more people than a rural farmer with regressive values."** Plausible and unquantified. Consumption footprints do scale with income and urban consumption patterns, but the claim as written compares two caricatures. No study was found that tests it directly [Guessing]. Keep it as a provocation.

**"Virtue signalling is a third mode of consumption."** An argument, not a finding. No study was sought or found on this framing. It is one of the thesis's sharpest lines and it is fine as an argument.

## 3.7 Part 6: the narcissism problem

**"Extreme self-awareness does not make us more empathetic; it restricts how we act and present ourselves."** Untested here. There is a large literature on self-presentation and validation-seeking online, but the agents were not asked to check it and the budget ran out before it could be added [could not verify]. The thesis's mechanism (performance replaces intent) is coherent and should be stated as a hypothesis.

**"They who perform for applause already lost their voice."** Poetry. Keep it.

## 3.8 Part 7: the solutions

**De-engineer your own conditioning.** Fine as advice. No evidence needed, and none claimed.

**Algorithmic transparency as a public right.** Already partly law in the EU. Platforms must state the main parameters of their recommender systems in plain language (Article 27); very large platforms must offer at least one option not based on profiling (Article 38); vetted researchers get data access (Article 40), with the implementing act in force since 29 October 2025 [DSA]. The thesis asks for something more: per-user disclosure of what content a person interacts with and its psychological effect. That is not law anywhere the agents could find, and it is what chapter 4 builds into the product instead of waiting for regulation.

**Data visualisation as the new storytelling; AI as the second brain of the world.** This is a design proposal, not a claim, and chapters 1, 2 and 4 take it literally: a model that reads everything and invents nothing, with a provenance flag on every record.

**The utopian counterfactual.** Untestable as written. The one verifiable piece is that love and connection are not fully monetisable, and the empathy research supports the thesis in an unexpected way: identical messages were rated more empathic when people believed a human wrote them, and people chose human support even when they rated the AI's words higher [Rubin 2025; Communications Psychology 2025]. A model can produce language that reads as care, sometimes better than a doctor's [Ayers 2023]. People still want the person. That is the thesis's best evidence and it is not in the thesis.

## 3.9 Part 8: the revolution clause

Gil Scott-Heron recorded "The Revolution Will Not Be Televised" in 1970 and again in 1971 [Likely]. The thesis's twist ("personalised to you to disagree with") is its own, and chapter 4 treats it as the design problem: a feed that is the same for everyone in a place is the direct inversion.

## 3.10 What survives: the strong version

Written in the thesis's own voice, using only what held:

Engagement-ranked feeds measurably favour anger and out-group hostility, and a different ranking measurably reduces it. Personalisation removes the shared reference points that any collective action needs. Empathy, as psychologists measure it, is not collapsing, but direct contact with nature and with place is thin, and that contact, not knowledge, is what predicts care. The things that cannot be ranked into existence are the felt ones: people prefer a human's care even when a machine's words score higher. The counterforce with evidence behind it is doing things with other people in a place. We owe each other everything. Most of the systems we live inside are built to help us forget that, and they can be rebuilt.

Everything cut from the thesis to get to that paragraph was either wrong (the physics), untestable (the metaphysics), or a 2011 statistic that reversed. Nothing essential was lost.
